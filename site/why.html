Why now

Government aid is shrinking. Philanthropy can't fill the gap. Organisations that know what they hold will be first in line.

Aid had its largest fall on record.

The funders who remain want partners who bring something to the table.

$40bn
less aid in 2025 than in 2024. Back to 2015 levels, in real terms.
¾
of the drop came from the United States, which cut its aid by 56.9%.
2026
The OECD expects a third straight year of decline.

  1. Philanthropy can't replace aid.

    Even the largest foundations say so. Private money is strongest where others won't go: early, risky, local work.

  2. Leverage beats replacement.

    Development banks now ask private funders to back the risky first stage. Bigger money follows what works. Every ask should say what it unlocks next.

  3. Funders want partners.

    Most give because a cause matches their values. They want to see what you bring, and what they get from working with you.

  4. Strings cost money.

    Tightly earmarked grants raise reporting costs and pull an organisation off its mission. How money comes matters as much as how much.
